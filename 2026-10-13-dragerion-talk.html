Players losing to Dragerion keep naming the same card, and it is not Dragerion

The daily survey counts decks. The people posting about losing to this one do not talk about the deck much at all — they name 神滅竜騎ネルガルザーク, a card inside it, and the two ways it arrives early. Meanwhile the deck lost the lead on 12 October, which is the sort of thing posts do not keep up with. One more thing got in the way of reading them: the current ranked season appears to be named after the same card.

This page reports what was said, not what is true These are eleven public posts, dated 29 September and 4, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13 October, found by Japanese keyword searches run on 12 and 13 October. None of them is a finding of this site. Where somebody says a card or a deck is strong, that is their assessment of their own games, not a measurement.

The search this site uses returns a handful of posts and then stops. It can show what individual people wrote and never what share of players wrote it. Handles are not reproduced. Its window also moves: the 29 September post below had already dropped out of the results by the time this page was checked on the evening of 13 October, so each post here is recorded from the reading that found it.

11 October does not have a clean answer. Dragerion read 12.6% and the Baraghiara loop line also read 12.6% — on 262 games that is thirty-three games each. Count Baraghiara as one family, adding the Cabbage entry at 2.3%, and it reads 14.9% and is ahead. So Dragerion led outright on one day, shared the top on the next by one reading and lost it by the other, and was second on the third.

None of the posts below mention any of that. They are about playing against the deck, which is a different thing from how often it appears.

The card they name is not the one on the box

On 13 October a player posting their Minogami Cup record went 1–4 on the first day of STAGE2 and said they met 火水闇ドラゲリオン twice. Their description of why they lost names no part of the Dragerion card: it was a combo using the cip of 学校男 or 堕魔 ドゥポイズ to put 神滅竜騎ネルガルザーク out early, and they had, in their words, no answer to it at all.

On 9 October a different player wrote that HADESドラゲリオン and ネルガルザーク were the two absurd power cards keeping them playing. That is two posts, four days apart, putting the same second card beside the first.

The earliest post found is older than the deck’s climb. On 29 September, when it was reading in the low single digits, someone who had come back to the game after a long gap wrote that they were piloting HADESドラゲリオン and ended with a one-line question about how many of its cards are simply too much. The Japanese there can be read as "too strong" or as "too badly designed"; the post does not say which, and this site is not going to pick one.

Both of those cards are already on this site 神滅竜騎ネルガルザーク and 堕魔 ドゥポイズ are both in the HADES Dragerion list on the three-decks page, recorded on 5 September. So the posts are not describing a new card; they are describing which card in a known list is doing the work. 学校男 is not a name this site has recorded, and it is given here as the post wrote it.

One player set out how they try to beat it

Also on 13 October: finish the game early, or cast オリオティス・ジャッジ and win immediately afterwards with speed attackers. Their own summary of how that goes was that they basically lose. That is one player's account of their own matches and not a matchup finding.

Half the search results are not about the deck at all

Searching the deck's name turns up posts that have nothing to do with it, because the current ranked season appears to be called ハデスドラゲリオンCUP. Players write the phrase to say when they did something, not what they played with.

Three posts make that unmistakable, because each names a different deck in the same breath. On 4 October someone reached New Division Legend in the Cup with 青緑バラギアラ. On 12 October someone reached All Division Master in the Cup and wrote their deck out in brackets: 赤白レイド. On 10 October a third reached New Division Master in the Cup, writing that it was their first in 32弾ぶり — the first in thirty-two sets — and that they had come back to both the format and the lists from scratch. The deck they describe afterwards is Raid as well.

Simple unfairness, hard on the opponent only. But play badly and you still lose. What surprised me most was how easy it is to accept losing with it. A player on 10 October — about Raid, in a post whose first line contains the word Dragerion. Paraphrased from Japanese.
This site has not confirmed the season name with the publisher The three posts above use ハデスドラゲリオンCUP as a period rather than a deck, which is what establishes the usage here. No publisher page this site can reach names the season, so it is reported as what players call it. Ranked season names have turned up only in the game before now.

The practical point stands either way: a keyword search on this deck's name is contaminated for as long as the season runs, and any count built from one would be wrong. That is a further reason the posts below are described one by one rather than totalled.

People are still picking it up

On 13 October someone wrote that this month's spending would go on building HADESドラゲリオン. On 11 October an All Division player wrote that they were reusing their New Division Cotton and Dragerion decks in that format, and wanted to build something that only exists there instead. Those are two people, and this site cannot say whether either is typical.

A fifth name turned up

The page on the lead change said this site had recorded four names for this deck. A Minogami Cup opponent list posted on 12 October used a fifth: クローシスダピコハデス, which welds together three of the others. The count on that page now reads five.

The naming page is about what this does to anyone comparing two sources. A fifth variant does not change its argument; it is simply more of the same problem.

A bug report, and a notice that does not quite match it

On 7 October a player set out a sequence step by step: play 双炎祭壇 アポロヌス・パトス, then HADESドラゲリオン without placing it underneath, have the opponent play あたりポンの助 to shut off the Dragerion effect, take −9000 from it, and then have it tucked under the Patos — at which point, they wrote, it breaks endlessly.

What the notice actually has The publisher's running known-issues notice, still dated 7 October when checked on 13 October, lists two entries for 双炎祭壇 アポロヌス・パトス. Both are about Twinpact dragons placed underneath it, and both sit in the section for issues already addressed. Neither describes the sequence in that post.

This site cannot say whether they share a cause, whether the posted sequence still happens, or whether the publisher knows about it. What can be said is that the post is six days old and nothing matching it has appeared in the notice. The same pattern — players describing a fault before the notice names it — happened at the end of September.

What this page is not

It is not a count and it is not a poll. Eleven posts are eleven people, and three of those turned out to be about a ranked season rather than a deck. Other posts the same searches returned are left out: one was spam, and several from 13 October refer to a streamer in connection with the deck in a way this site could not establish, so they are not described here rather than guessed at.

Nothing here says the deck is strong or weak. The daily figures above say how often people met it, and they are already pointing somewhere else.
