PLAY’S · META · 26 Oct 2026

A deck that was on twenty-nine charts in a row is not on the last three

《“B-零朱”レイド》 was changed at the 22 October maintenance. On the three full days of play since, 赤白レイド is not a named slice on the daily ranked survey — it was one on all twenty-nine days before. 赤白轟轟轟(レイド無し), the build the chart names without that card, has gone 11.9, 11.6, 16.6 against a ceiling of 3.6 on the days before it. But the two colours’ combined share has not grown: it sits inside the band it already held, in the lower half of it. What the chart records is a substitution, and it cannot say what caused one.

What this page is reading, and when it read it This is the data side. The player side — what people posted about the deck in the same week — is a separate page from 25 October, and this one links to it rather than repeating it. They are two different kinds of evidence that happen to point the same way.

Thirty-two days are read: 24 September to 25 October 2026, with no gaps. Every figure was read off a published pie chart by hand, which is the step on this site most likely to go wrong. The 25 October chart, the last one here, was read on the morning of the 26th: 290 games, fourteen labels and fourteen wedges, summing to 100.0.

The 22 October maintenance changed more than a dozen things at once, by the publisher’s own list of what it did. Three of them could plausibly change which decks people meet: the two card abilities, the seventh collaboration’s set and deck builders going on sale, and its Legend Battle opening — which is a separate mode from the ranked ladder these readings come from. The deck-builder card pool changed too, from eleven products to fourteen. Anything that moves from the 22nd on has several candidate causes and this survey cannot separate them.

One thing is at least held still: one ranked season covers the whole window — オラマッハ・ザ・ジョニーカップ, 16 October to 15 November, so no season boundary falls inside it. Days before the 16th are in a different season and are not comparable to it on that count alone.

This site’s own readings, the seven days around the change. The red rule marks the change: everything below it was played under the new card text. The 22nd is about eight hours of play — the game was down from 23:15 the night before until 16:00 that afternoon — so its games are all under the new text and there are only forty-six of them. That chart also names nineteen slices and has no unnamed bucket at all, against eleven slices the day before: at forty-six games most decks are one or two, and the survey labelled all of them. The 4.3 in the Raid-less column that day is two games, and the day is after the change, so that build’s ceiling before the change is the 3.6 of 17 October — six games out of 166. The “pair” column adds the two red/white columns and counts an unnamed build as nothing, so each figure is a floor and not a total.

赤白レイド was a named slice on twenty-nine consecutive days, 24 September to 22 October. Its readings in that run go from 6.5 on the part-day of the 22nd to 24.4 on 24 September, which is a partial six-column read. It was the chart’s leading named deck on thirteen of the thirty-two days and tied for the lead on one more — more often than any other deck in the series; ジョーカーズ is next with seven and two. Counting only the twenty-five days read in full, it led eleven and tied one against ジョーカーズ’s six and two.

Then nothing, three days running, across samples of 227, 146 and 290.

Of the eight decks with a long run, seven are still on the chart

Twenty-five of the thirty-two days are full reads, where every slice was recorded. Eight decks are named on twenty or more of those twenty-five. Six of them — ジョーカーズ, バラギアラループ, ゼニス天門, 赤単バルガ, HADESドラゲリオン and コットンゼニス — have never missed a full-read day. 青魔導具 has missed exactly one, 18 October, and is on all three charts since the change.

赤白レイド is the eighth, and the only one of them absent from the last three charts. All three of its absences are those three days.

“Not a named slice” is not zero, and the ceiling here is the chart’s own

A deck under the survey’s labelling cutoff falls into the unnamed bucket rather than leaving the format. That bucket reads 13.4, 8.2 and 11.9 on the three days — about 30, 12 and 35 games. So the honest statement is that the deck is below the chart’s smallest named slice, not that it is gone:

  • 23 October — smallest named slice 2.6, which is about six games in 227. So at most five of the day’s games.
  • 24 October — smallest named slice 2.1, about three games in 146. At most two.
  • 25 October — smallest named slice 2.1, about six games in 290. At most five.

And that ceiling is inferred, not published. This site does not know the survey’s labelling rule. It is not a fixed number of slices — the three days name ten, twelve and thirteen decks plus the bucket, and the 22nd names nineteen with no bucket. It does not look like a flat percentage either: 2.1 is named on two of the three days, while the bucket on the 25th holds about thirty-five games. The figures above are read off each chart’s own smallest label and are worth exactly that much.

The colours did not leave. One of their two builds replaced the other

Add the two red/white columns together on every full-read day and the series reads differently from the headline. Across the twenty-one full days before the change that floor runs from 10.7 to 27.7, averaging about 18. Since the change it reads 11.9, 11.6 and 16.6, averaging about 13.

All three fall inside the range the pair already occupied, in its lower half. The part-day of the 22nd, at 10.8, is the second-lowest figure in the whole full-read series. The chart does not show the colour pair going away. What it shows is which of the two builds gets a label, and that has changed hands, with the pair’s total near the bottom of what it has held before — and three days cannot tell the bottom of a known band from a new level.

The size of the move is ordinary here. The timing is not

赤白轟轟轟(レイド無し) went 4.3 on the part-day to 16.6 on the 25th, a move of 12.3 points in four days. Measured against this survey’s own volatility that is not remarkable. The two widest three-day swings in the series are both larger and both happened with nothing altered in the game:

  • 赤白レイド read 24.1, 15.0, 8.3 across 17 to 19 October — 15.8 points.
  • ジョーカーズ read 10.8, 11.4, 26.5 across 16 to 18 October — 15.7 points.

So the rise on its own needs no explanation; this chart produces moves that size on quiet weeks. What has no precedent in these thirty-two days is a run of that length ending at all, and this one ends on the first full day under the new card text. That is the whole of the finding, and it is a coincidence of dates until something else confirms it.

The chart’s other build named for omitting the card did not move

赤単轟轟轟(レイド無し) — mono-red rather than red/white, and named the same way for leaving the card out — reads 2.1, 3.6, 2.6, 2.3, 2.5, 3.8 and 2.4 on the seven full days it was named before the change, 4.3 on the part-day, then 2.6, 2.1, 2.8. It is flat across the change.

That is a fact about the chart and not an explanation of one. At two to six games a day the column is inside its own noise either way, and nothing in this data says why a deck did or did not move.

What the players said is on another page

Twenty-one posts from 19 to 25 October were collected and written up separately, as talk and not as fact: five describe the deck as gone, three name the Raid-less build as what replaced it, two are from people still playing it, and the largest post in the set argues the complaints are overblown. That page deliberately stopped short of the result this one reports, and said so at the time.

The two should not be stacked into one piece of evidence. A search that returns between seven and eleven posts is not a sample of players, and a survey of ranked games is not a survey of opinions. They agree here; that is worth noting once and not leaning on.

What this page is not

It is not a verdict on the change. The notice of 20 October states the change as balance adjustment and names no deck, no tournament and no figure, so there is no stated intent to measure the chart against. A page saying the change worked would be inventing the goal first and then scoring it.

It is not a claim that the change caused this. Three things started on the same afternoon, and the survey has one column for the outcome and none for the cause.

The survey counts decks met in ranked games, not players. One build taking another’s place on a pie chart is not evidence that the same people rebuilt the same deck; the chart has no way to follow anybody from one day to the next.

And it is three days. The standing example on this site is ジョーカーズ reading 11.4, then 26.5, then 14.3, then 22.7, then 20.8 across five days with the game untouched. A week is the shortest window this site has been willing to call a move. Week five of these readings covers 22 to 28 October and is not written yet; week four is the most recent one that is.

Correction, 26 October — a figure this site published earlier the same day The dated note added to the card-change page this morning said 赤白轟轟轟(レイド無し) had a best of “4.3 on the nine days it appeared before the change”. The 4.3 is from 22 October, which is after the change, not before it — play resumed at 16:00 that day with the new card text, as that same page states two paragraphs further down. Eight of those nine days are before the change and their ceiling is 3.6.

The 4.3 is also the weaker number of the two: on a 46-game day it is two games, where 3.6 on 17 October is six games out of 166. The note has been corrected on that page and in this site’s data file, and the figure used throughout this page is 3.6.
