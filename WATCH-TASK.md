# Where to look

Fixed list. Checking these in order takes a few minutes and catches almost everything.
**Two things were missed in September by not having this written down:** a Magic collaboration
announced through a shop page, and a pre-Grand Prix column that only appears under /special/.

## Every check

| Source | URL | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| PLAY'S official X | `@dmps_info` | Cards, events, maintenance. Fastest, but posts vanish from the timeline within a day |

it, say so in the article — that distinction has already mattered once, over a new-player offer that
was advertised but never documented.

## Order of checking

1. `@dmps_info` — anything new since last time
2. PLAY'S news index — does a documented version exist yet
3. `@t2duema`
4. Columns and Mall, if it has been a few days
5. `@mtgjp`, if anything crossover-shaped is moving
6. Environment data, if it has been about a week

**Stop when nothing is new.** The official accounts post one or two things a day. Checking again
twenty minutes later returns the same answer.

### The notice slot is usually 17:00 JST

**Usually. Read the next section before relying on this one** — this heading said "is 17:00" until
31 October, and two October notices are not at 17:00.

Seven October notices were timed when this section was written and all seven carried **5:00 PM**:
the four of 20 October (16416, 16418, 16420, 16454) and the three of 19 October (16410, 16412,
16414). X carries the same announcements at the same minute — the two card-change posts are 08:00
and 08:01 UTC, which is 17:00 and 17:01 JST.

**A check made before 17:00 is a check on yesterday.** Ask the day's question after 17:00. If
something is being published, check again after 17:00 however recent the earlier look was — a check
at 16:56 missed four notices by four minutes and put a false claim on the site. **That holds. What
does not hold is treating 17:00 as the last check of the day.**

### 17:00 is the usual slot, not the only one

**The slot was never universal, and this file said it was.** Twenty-two October notices have now had
their timestamps read. **Ten carry 5:00 PM. Twelve do not.**

| Time | Count | Notices |
|---|---|---|
| **4:00 PM** | 9 | `16285` `16422` `16424` `16426` `16430` `16433` `16435` `16439` `16481`, all 22 October |
| 5:00 PM | 10 | the seven of 19–20 October, plus `16437` `16477` (26th) and `16479` (29th) |
| **6:00 PM** | 1 | `16522`, 23 October |
| **11:00 PM** | 1 | `16475`, 30 October |
| **12:00 AM** | 1 | `16308`, 16 October |

**Nine of the twelve exceptions are a single day**: 22 October, the day the seventh collaboration
opened, where everything went up at 16:00 together. That is the shape to expect — **a day with a
launch on it runs on the launch's clock, not the daily slot.**

The earlier rule was written from seven notices of two adjacent days and then stated as a law. The
counterexamples were all reachable the whole time, several of them on the second page of the index
this file also said did not exist. **Read the timestamp on every notice and keep this table honest;
do not restate the pattern from memory.**

**30 October 2026.** `news/16475`, the signed-card exchange, is timestamped **11:00 PM**. The
collaboration stream began at 21:00; the X posts announcing what came out of it run **22:16 to
22:42**, and the notice followed at 23:00, eighteen minutes after the last of them. **When the
stream ended is not stated anywhere**, so the notice is placed against the posts, not against the
broadcast.

So the rule above holds for the daily slot and **does not** hold for anything tied to an event:

- **A notice attached to a broadcast goes up after the broadcast, not at 17:00.** Both streams on
  record work this way: 12 October announced a collaboration mid-broadcast, and 30 October produced
  five posts and a notice between 22:16 and 23:00.
- **The X run and the notice can be hours apart, in either direction.** On 29 October the SP Rule
  Match notice carries 5:00 PM and the X posts for it did not start until **19:00**. On 30 October
  the posts came first and the notice followed at 23:00.
- So: **when a stream or a timed event is scheduled, check again after it ends**, not at 17:00 and
  not the next morning. A check at 20:00 on the 30th would have been honest and useless: it would
  have missed the notice and all five of the posts that followed the stream. **"Nothing new as of
  20:00" is a claim that stays true; "nothing new on the 30th" would have been false.**

### The news index paginates, and this file said it did not

**Corrected 31 October 2026. The section below was wrong, and the site has been working around a
limit that does not exist.**

`https://dmps.takaratomy.co.jp/news/page/2` returns **a different ten items**, and the pagination
runs to **page 169**; `page/170` is a 404. So the index is a **full archive**, not a window. What
fails is the query-string forms: `?pg=2` and `?page=2` both return page one, which is what was
tested on 26 October, and the conclusion was drawn from that.

| Form | Result |
|---|---|
| `/news/page/2` | **works** — different ten |
| `/news/page/2/` | **works** — same as above |
| `/news/?page=2` | returns page one |
| `/news/?pg=2` | returns page one |

**Use `/news/page/N`.** The ID-probing described below is not needed and never was: `news/16308`
was always two clicks away. Anything this site has recorded as "not on the news index" on the
strength of the front page should be re-checked against the paginated index before it is repeated —
**that phrasing appears on published pages.**

**That re-check was done on 31 October**, across pages 1–6 of the index, sixty notices, back past
the Railgun collaboration. Every published "X only / no news-index page" claim on this site survived
it except one:

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 配信者王決定戦バトルロイヤル2026, 12 October | no notice — **holds** |
| The Legend Battle ending unlocked, 27 October | no notice — **holds** |
| The collaboration stream, 30 October | no notice — **holds** |
| 神アート絆-03 / -04 on the PLAY'S index | no notice — **holds** |
| The collaboration playmat | no notice — **holds** |
| **The returning scenarios** | **WRONG — `news/16426`, 22 October** |

So the front-page habit cost exactly one error, which is luck rather than method. Check the
paginated index before writing that something is not documented.

### What the front page shows, and the claim that was built on it

**Found on 26 October.** The ranked season notice for the season that started 16 October
(`news/16308`, 「【ランクマッチ】オラマッハ・ザ・ジョニーカップ開催！」) is **not on
`dmps.takaratomy.co.jp/news/` at all.** The front page listed ten items, oldest of them
`news/16285`, and `?pg=2`, `?page=2` and `/page/2/` all return the same ten.

So the index is a window, not an archive, and a notice ten items old is invisible from it.
Two consequences:

- **"It is not on the news index" is a claim about the front page, nothing more.** Say which
  you mean. The calendar carried "Documented on the news index" for that season with no
  notice number, which is how the gap stayed open.
- **A notice page stays reachable by number.** `news/16308` loads fine. Probing a range of
  numbers found it: of 171 IDs tried between 16290 and 16460, only 17 returned 200. **That probe
  was unnecessary** — see the correction above; walking `/news/page/N` finds the same notice
  without guessing. Record the number in the calendar the first time either way.

### Top ordering returns posts from previous years

**25 October 2026.** A search for `デュエプレ (弱体 OR ナーフ)` in **top** ordering returned nine
posts. Three were not from this year: one from **2021**, one from **2025** and one from July. X
shows a bare `10月26日` for an old post and the full date only sometimes, so a quick read makes a
five-year-old complaint look like today's.

**Read the year off the `datetime` attribute, every time.** Latest ordering is safe — everything in
it is recent — but top ordering is ranked by engagement over all time, which is exactly why the
large posts worth citing turn up there.

Reporting a 2021 post as current reaction would be the worst version of the mistake this section
already warns about, because the engagement figure would be real and the context gone.

### X timestamps are UTC

The `datetime` attribute on a post is UTC; add nine hours. `2026-10-20T12:30` went up at 21:30 JST.
Getting this wrong moves a post across a day boundary.

### The publisher corrects its own images — look for 訂正

Two in one day on 20 October:

- The deck-builder notice opens with an apology for the 「配信者王決定戦」 broadcast of 12 October:
  the box front shown for DMPC-18 was wrong. The notice carries the corrected art.
- An X post at 21:30 JST corrected the hat on 鈴原るる's skin in an earlier image.

**Search the account for 【訂正】 before trusting anything read off an image.**

### A product code is a text fact — wait for the text

DMPC-18 was read off box art as 鈴原るる and published in two files. The deck-builder notice gives
DMPC-18 as 栞葉るり and 鈴原るる as DMPC-16. Two other codes were recorded as illegible when they
were simply not yet published as text; all five arrived at once in the product notice.

Mark an image-read code **provisional**, and go back to it when the product notice lands.

## X announces, the product page documents

**Three articles written on 11–12 September all needed correcting, and all for the same reason:**
they were written from the announcement post alone.

| Article | What was missing |
|---|---|
| Kami Art × Magic | Price, closing date, product codes, contents. The article said the closing date "had not been announced" — it had |
| Grand Prix viewing guide | The columnist's name, while the article itself argued for naming sources |
| B-Reishu Raid | The card's tournament submission rate, which was the evidence behind the complaint being quoted |

**X posts sell. Product pages and wikis document.** A reveal post gives a name, a date and a hook.
It does not give price, run length, product code, or contents — and those are what a reader needs
to understand what the thing actually is.

### Before publishing anything product-shaped, check

1. `dm.takaratomy.co.jp/product/` or the PLAY'S equivalent — official spec page
2. `dmwiki.net` — has product codes, price, contents, usually within a day
3. The shop listing, if it is a Mall exclusive — purchase limits and the close date

### Before publishing anything card-shaped, check

1. Card text against **two** sources minimum — the card database, the wiki, Kamigame's evaluation page
2. **Whether the card has a tournament record.** Submission rates from Battle Arena and sanctioned
   events are published and are the strongest evidence available
3. **Interactions between its own abilities.** The Raid article claimed the cheap copy would
   self-destruct and could be waited out; its own protection ability prevents that. Reading each
   ability separately is not the same as reading the card

## Scroll past the headline

On Grand Prix day the official account posted the tournament result and the set teaser — and
**four card reveals underneath them**. Checking the top of the timeline and stopping there missed
all four, and an article went out describing the set from the wrong half of it.

**A big story pushes the ordinary posts down, it does not replace them.** On any day with a major announcement, scroll further than usual, not less — with real input
scrolling, see below.

### Card reveals come through at least four channels

| Channel | Note |
|---|---|
| `@dmps_info` | Roughly one an hour during a reveal run |
| Corocoro Online | Has its own exclusives — not mirrored on the official account |
| Japanese guide sites | Also given exclusives; their set roundups list every card known so far |
| **Official YouTube** | **A whole card was shown here and nowhere else.** Not checked until 15 Sep |

### Scrolling X: use real input, not scripted scrolling

**This was wrong for most of a day and cost two missed reveals.** Scripted scrolling — calling
`window.scrollTo` and friends from the console — moves the page but **does not make X load the next
batch.** The loader never fires, no follow-up request is sent, and the timeline sits at six or seven
posts however long you scroll. It looks exactly like a hard cap. It is not one.

**Use real input events instead** — the browser tool's own scroll action, which the page receives as
a genuine wheel event. Then it pages normally:

```
scripted scrolling      7 posts, page height frozen at 7205
real input scrolling    7 → 13 → 16 → 19, height 7205 → 12480, still climbing
```

So **X can be read back through properly**, and the official account is usable as a primary source
on its own. Alternate scrolling with collecting, because only about ten posts stay mounted at once —
anything scrolled past is discarded from the page.

#### The same is true of search, and this file said otherwise for weeks

**1 November 2026.** The fix above was found for the timeline and **never retested against search**.
Both this file and `ARTICLE-QUEUE.md` went on saying a search "returns 6–11 posts and stops" and
that this was "not fixable" and "tested repeatedly". Retested with real input scrolling, one query
in latest ordering:

```
initial load   17 posts
scroll  1      20
scroll  2      23
scroll  3      34
scroll  4      40   — and still climbing when the test was stopped
```

Forty posts spanning **1 hour 46 minutes**, all of them this year. The cap was the scripted-scroll
artefact all along; the entry describing it survived its own correction because nobody re-ran the
query the fix applied to.

**What this changes.** A sample of forty over a stated window is a different instrument from a
sample of eleven. It is still a *search* — it returns what the index gives for that term, not every
post — so claims about "players" in general stay out. But **counting over a window is now worth
doing**: "of the 40 posts this query returned between 22:45 and 00:31, N raise X" is a sentence
this site can stand behind, and could not before.

**Mounted is not collected.** Only about twenty articles stay in the DOM, so collect after every
scroll and keep a cumulative set keyed on the `datetime` attribute, or the count will look flat
while the page is actually paging.

### The guide site's exclusive slot has a pattern

Across 14, 15 and 16 September it posted **one card a day**, and **all three were PLAY'S originals
with no paper printing.** If that holds, the slot exists for cards that cannot be looked up anywhere
else — which makes it the highest-value thing on this list to check daily, not the lowest.

A paper card revealed on X can be cross-checked against paper databases. **An original revealed
there cannot be checked anywhere.** Miss it and it is simply absent from your coverage.

### Still check a roundup afterwards

Not because X is capped, but because **reveals do not all go to X.** Corocoro Online, Japanese guide
sites and the official YouTube channel each carry cards that appear nowhere else. A guide site's set
roundup aggregates all four and is the fastest way to see what you have missed.

### Names disagree between sources

The same card appeared as <span class="jp">凶鬼卍号</span> on the official account and
<span class="jp">凶鬼09号</span> on a guide site. **Where two secondary sources disagree, read the
card image, or say plainly that they disagree.** Do not pick one silently.

## The calendar page goes stale silently

`2026-08-16-calendar.md` is the one page whose whole value is being current, and **it is the one
page that gives no sign when it is not.** A wrong card article gets corrected because someone
notices. A calendar missing three weeks of dates just looks like a calendar.

**Check it whenever a date is announced anywhere**, and at minimum:

- when a set release date is given
- when an event, stream slot or maintenance window is announced
- when a sale or product window closes
- **before writing any article that mentions a date** — if it is not on the calendar, add it there too

### What that page is for

One page an English reader can open to see what is coming for both games. It fails the moment a
reader checks it and then finds out elsewhere that something happened last week.

## What to record when something turns up

- Date and time of the post, and which account
- Whether a news-index page exists or it is X only
- Card names copied, never retyped — 課 and 誣 are different characters (「誣」の頂 ウェディング・イノセンス)
- Engagement figures if the reaction is the story
- The URL, for the sources list
