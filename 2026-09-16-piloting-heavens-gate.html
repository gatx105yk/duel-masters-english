How to actually play the Heaven's Gate precon

You bought the Nature/Light preconstructed deck and the curve looks broken. There is nothing at four or five. That looks like a broken curve and is not one — the deck is built to arrive early anyway, and this is how.

The two cards the deck is built on

Huge Blueprint · two mana 巨大設計図

Pulls up to four creatures costing seven or more out of your deck and into your hand.

On its own it does nothing to the board. What it does is guarantee that the expensive half of your deck is in your hand by turn two or three.

Heaven's Gate · six mana ヘブンズ・ゲート

Puts Light blockers straight from your hand into play, free, with no limit on what they cost. The more expensive the blocker, the better the deal.

It is also a Shield Trigger, so it can go off while you are being attacked rather than on your own turn.

That is the whole deck. Blueprint finds the big creatures; the Gate skips their cost. Everything else supports one of those two jobs.

You have eight turn-two plays, not four

Blueprint is only half of it. The seven-cost Nature creature in the list is a Twinpact, and its spell half costs two and ramps you.

Ichigotchi Tank / Let's Goichigo · seven, or two イチゴッチ・タンク／レッツ・ゴイチゴ

As a creature: seven mana, 12000 power, Triple Breaker.

As a spell: two mana, put the top card of your deck into your mana zone.

So the early game is eight two-drops across four card slots — four that find your big creatures and four that accelerate you toward casting them. Which one you want depends on whether your hand already has the expensive half of the deck in it.

And ramping is what gets you there before turn six Accelerate on two and three and you can be at five mana on turn four. That matters because Stargaze Gate — the spell half of the nine-cost creature — costs five and cheats out a blocker on its own. Decks built on this archetype in the wider format are known for starting on turn four rather than waiting for the Gate.

The Gate is the ceiling, not the plan. You want to be doing something before it arrives, and the deck gives you the cards to.

Half the deck is two cards in one

Six of the twelve entries are Twinpacts: a creature on one half, a spell on the other, two different costs printed on the same card.

This is what stops the deck dying to its own curve. A nine-cost creature in your opening hand is a dead card in most decks. Here it is also a five-cost spell.

Read the right-hand column again: 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. That is the curve the deck actually plays on. The creature costs are what those cards become later, not what you pay early.

Stargaze Gate, the spell half of the nine-drop, is worth knowing specifically: it is a cheaper Heaven's Gate that only cheats out one blocker and has no Shield Trigger. When you have the Gate you use the Gate; when you do not, this is the backup.

The thing that will catch you out

Seven cards is the whole battle zone PLAY'S caps the battle zone at seven, and when it is full, anything an effect tries to put into play goes to the graveyard instead.

A deck that cheats two creatures in at a time can hit that ceiling. If your board is nearly full, a Heaven's Gate off a broken shield can bin the blockers rather than deploy them. Paper has no such limit and this interaction does not exist there.

And one coming for it

Set 37 contains a nine-cost Light blocker that, on arrival and again whenever one of your creatures wins a battle, draws a card and fetches another blocker out of your hand.

Nine mana is a price this deck never pays — it cheats blockers in for free, and this is a blocker. Put it in off the Gate and it puts a third one out behind it.

Which is when the board cap starts to bite Chaining blockers into a seven-card battle zone runs out of room quickly, and a full board sends the next arrival to the graveyard instead. This is the deck most likely to find that limit.

And the thing coming against it

Set 37 contains a four-cost card that returns every creature with Blocker to its owner's hand. A deck whose entire defence is blockers is exactly what that is aimed at.

It is not released yet, and the deck you have bought is an All Division deck, so nothing rotates away underneath it. But if this becomes hard to win with in October, that card is why.

What this guide does not cover

I have not played this deck Everything above is read off the card text and the decklist — what the cards do, how the costs fit together, and which rules interact with them. It is not play experience.

Questions like when to hold the Gate for defence instead of casting it, which blocker to fetch against which opponent, or whether to mulligan a hand with two nine-drops and no Blueprint need someone who has actually piloted it. If that is you, the comments are open.
