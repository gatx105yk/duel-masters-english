Set 37's first card is a free spell that clears the board

Revealed this evening through Corocoro Online rather than the official account — a Fire spell that costs nothing under one condition, and removes every small creature the opponent controls.

Izanto

《“閃忍勝”威斬斗》 · Set 37 · Very Rare
Type Spell Civ Fire Cost 6 Exists on paper Yes, since 2019
Master G・G・GIf this card is the only thing in your hand, cast it without paying. EffectDestroys every creature the opponent controls with power 3000 or less. EffectDraws you a card if you have a Fire card in your mana zone.

The keyword is a stricter version of one already in the format

The most-played deck in the game runs a card with G・G・G, which switches on when your hand is down to one card or fewer. This one is Master G・G・G, and the condition is tighter: the spell must be the only card you are holding.

Not "one or fewer" — this one, alone. An empty hand does not qualify, because the spell has to be in it.

Which makes it an emptied-hand payoff, not an emptied-hand condition Fire decks in this game run their hand to nothing by design. A card that rewards you for arriving at exactly one card left — and specifically this card — asks you to sequence your turn so that it is the last thing standing.

What it actually does

Power 3000 or less is a wide net in this format. Two-cost and three-cost creatures mostly sit under that line, which is where aggressive decks keep their early board and where several formats' worth of disruption creatures live.

At six mana it is not cheap. At zero mana, with a board wipe and a card attached, it is a different proposition entirely — and the condition for that is one a Fire deck reaches on its own.

It is a 2019 paper card

The original appeared in a 2019 paper set built around turning the signature attacks of existing creatures into spells. Corocoro covered that set at the time, across two issues, and has pulled out its own back numbers to say so.

That fits what set 37 appears to be doing. The set's headline cards are reprints of 2018 paper cards, and its name is lifted from a paper release of the same era. This is another one.
