Glossary

The Japanese terms you run into watching Duel Masters content, with what they mean and — where there is one — the nearest Magic equivalent. Most of these have never been written down in English.

Basics Keywords Formats Set codes Deck names

The basics

Mana zoneマナゾーン

There are no land cards. Once per turn you put one card from your hand face-up into the mana zone, permanently, and it counts as one mana. Any card can do this.

vs Magic: lands are free; here every mana costs you a real card. Full explanation.

Chargeチャージ

The act of putting a card into the mana zone. Once per turn, before you attack.

Civilization文明

The five colours, plus Zero. In PLAY'S, a civilization is either present in your mana zone or it is not — the count does not matter. One Water card in the mana zone lets you cast any number of Water cards, mana permitting.

vs Magic: colour is a yes/no question rather than a quantity. This is the single biggest rules difference and it is why five-civilization decks are viable.

Shieldsシールド

Five face-down cards instead of a life total. A creature connects, one shield breaks, and the broken shield goes into the defender's hand. Break all five and one more attack wins the game.

vs Magic: damage is not permanent — attacking gives your opponent cards. Why this makes players reluctant to attack.

Direct attackダイレクトアタック

Attacking a player with no shields left. This is how games are won.

Breakerブレイカー

How many shields a creature breaks per attack. W-Breaker is two, T-Breaker three, World Breaker all of them.

Keywords

Shield TriggerS・トリガー

The defining mechanic. When a shield with this keyword breaks and enters your hand, you may cast it immediately without paying its cost, during your opponent's turn.

vs Magic: like an instant, except it comes from your life total, costs nothing, and your opponent triggered it by attacking you.

S-BackS・バック

A spell that, when cast from hand, goes face-up on top of one of your own shields instead of to the graveyard — so it can be used a second time when that shield breaks.

Blockerブロッカー

A keyword, not a default ability. Only creatures with Blocker can block. Everything else stands there while attacks go past.

vs Magic: the opposite default — in Magic any untapped creature can block. This is why boards do not gum up the way Magic boards do.

Speed Attackerスピードアタッカー

Can attack the turn it arrives. Magic calls this haste.

Mach Fighterマッハファイター

Can attack a creature the turn it arrives, but not a player. A restricted haste that exists to kill things without opening shields.

Revolution Change革命チェンジ

When a qualifying creature attacks, you may swap it with a Revolution Change card from your hand. The new creature continues the attack. It arrives without being summoned, for no mana, already attacking.

vs Magic: there is no equivalent. Attacking becomes the cost of cheating something into play.

GR summonGR召喚

A separate twelve-card zone outside your deck. When an effect calls for a GR summon, you turn over the top card of that zone and put it into play. You do not choose which.

vs Magic: a second library, played from the top, at random. Closest thing is the command zone, but that is deterministic.

Super-dimension超次元

Another zone outside the deck, holding Psychic creatures. Unlike GR, cards here are summoned deterministically — an effect names what it brings out.

Mana Armsマナ武装

A conditional ability that switches on when your mana zone contains a specified number of cards of a given civilization. Rewards committing to one colour.

G-ZeroG・ゼロ

Cast this card for free if a stated board condition is met.

Slayerスレイヤー

Destroys any creature it fights, whatever the power. Magic's deathtouch.

Just Diverジャストダイバー

Untargetable and unblockable until the start of your next turn. Protects a creature for exactly one rotation.

Formats and regulation

New Division · All DivisionND · AD

ND rotates — older sets fall out over time. AD holds everything. Separate ranked ladders, separate metagames, separate tier lists. The Grand Prix uses ND.

vs Magic: Standard versus Legacy, roughly — except both run inside the same client with their own ladders.

Hall of Fame殿堂

The paper game's restriction system. Five categories, not two:

プレミアム殿堂コンビ — two named cards cannot share a deck; four of each is legal apart
プレミアム殿堂超次元コンビ — same idea for the super-dimension zone
プレミアム殿堂 — zero copies
殿堂入り — one copy
解除 — unrestricted, four copies

Cards move both ways. A card dropping from Premium to Hall of Fame goes from zero copies to one — that is a loosening, and it is easy to misread as a restriction. Worked example.

DP Hall of FameDP殿堂

PLAY'S has its own restriction list, separate from paper. It also does something paper cannot: rewrite a card's text. Nerfs shipped in January, February, March, April and June of 2026.

Prohibited cards使用禁止カード

Separate from the Hall of Fame and unchanging. Two entries: one red-backed 2004 promo, and Duel Masters cards in any language other than Japanese. The entire 2002–2006 English print run is unusable in sanctioned Japanese play.

Set codes

The prefix tells you what a product is before you know anything else about it.

CodeGameWhat it is
DM__-RP_PaperNumbered regular set — defines the format
DM__-EX_PaperExtra set — collaborations, compilations
DM__-SD_ / BD_PaperPreconstructed decks
DMPP-__PLAY'SRegular set. New one roughly every two months
DMPX-__PLAY'SNot a regular set — special release, sits outside the numbering

DMPB-__PLAY'SAnniversary set. Reprints here re-enter ND legality for a year

If you have been counting PLAY'S sets and wondered where one went, a DMPX release is usually the answer — it does not advance the numbered line.

Reading deck names

Japanese deck names are civilizations plus a key card. Learn six characters and most names decode themselves.

光 Light
水 Water
闇 Darkness
火 Fire
自然 Nature
零 Zero

単 means mono-, as in 火単 — mono-Fire. 5c means all five. So 火水闇ドラゲリオン is Fire/Water/Darkness built on Dragerion.
