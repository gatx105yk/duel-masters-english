Eight preconstructed decks for the format nobody covers

Announced during the Grand Prix broadcast: four new Ultimate All Division Decks, plus the four from 2025 returning to sale. All Division is the format that holds every card ever released, and it gets almost no attention — in Japanese, and none at all in English.

All four lists are below, counts included. Every one totals forty, which is the check that they were read correctly. Card effects are described rather than reproduced.

The four new ones

Ultimate All Division Deck 2026

Fire/Light Apollonus 火光アポロヌス · DMPD-21

An evolution deck. Evolution creatures stack onto something you already control and never suffer summoning sickness, so they hit the turn they arrive — which is what makes this shape aggressive rather than slow.

Yes, that says ten copies Four-of is the normal limit. Centuria Dragon overrides it on the card itself — its own text allows five or more in a deck. It is a PLAY'S original, a four-cost dragon that fetches another copy of itself from your deck when it arrives, so every one you draw pulls the next. Ten is not a misprint; it is the point of the card.

The key card has two faces. One is a Phoenix Field — a non-creature permanent that reduces what your fire dragons cost. Put dragons underneath it, and once three are stacked there it flips into the Phoenix proper. The finisher works by consuming what is underneath: at three it clears the opponent's shields outright; stack more and it gets worse for them, up to adding World Breaker and shield burn, which shuts off Shield Triggers entirely.

The rest is a support shell. Pappi Rappy digs for the Phoenix, Centuria Dragon supplies bodies to stack under it, and Mixel, Oriotis Judge, DNA Spark and Holy are there to survive long enough — the first two punish cheating and ramping, the last two defend.

Effects for Yūai no Tenbin and Balkerios Dragon have not been checked and are not described.

Fire/Darkness Dormageddon 火闇ドルマゲドン · DMPD-22

Built on the Forbidden. The card starts in play before the game does, held shut by seals; strip them off and it flips into something enormous.

The only card in the game's history that is on the board from turn zero.

What is in the Dormageddon deck

This one does not have forty cards. It has forty plus one that sits outside them.

The Forbidden is not part of your deck Forbidden Star — The Last Day of the World FORBIDDEN STAR 〜世界最後の日〜 is a separate card that starts the game already in the battle zone, held shut by four seals. It does not count towards your forty, and you may only run one card of its kind — the other Forbidden cards are mutually exclusive with it.

Removing a seal is deliberately hard. One per turn at most, and only by summoning a Darkness or Fire command of cost five or more, or putting one out with Invasion ZERO. Nothing else opens it.

It also taxes your deckbuilding while it sits there: creatures that are not commands, Initials or Forbidden cards cannot attack at all. Filling the deck with unrelated cards does not work, which is why the product is built the way it is.

Each seal does something different

The four seals sit at the corners, and which one you remove decides what fires:

  • Reduce an opposing creature's power by 1111
  • Give one of your creatures Power Attacker +2222
  • Give one of your creatures Slayer
  • And the fourth is the detonation itself

You choose the order, and the order is a real decision. Take the detonating seal off early and you cannot detonate at all. The Slayer grant is the one players rate highest — small numbers, but a Slayer kills whatever it fights regardless of power, which turns a blocker or a battle-triggering creature into removal that cannot fail.

And then it flips

Strip all four and it detonates into Dormageddon X, a creature with power 999999, T-Breaker, and the ability to survive removal twice. On arrival it seals every creature the opponent controls — and sealed this way, they leave the game entirely rather than going to the graveyard, so nothing brings them back.

The catch: if it does get removed, you lose.

Fire/Water Dogiragon Buster 火水ドギラゴン剣 · DMPD-23

The Revolution Change deck. Attack with something small, swap it for this out of your hand mid-attack, and the attack carries on with the big creature instead. No mana paid.

Nature/Light Giant Heaven's Gate 巨大天門 · DMPD-24

Cheats Light blockers into play for free off a single spell, then wins from behind a wall. The same engine sitting third in the current New Division metagame — see the Zenith list — built here out of the whole card pool instead.

Look at the second numbers. Six of the twelve entries are Twinpacts — a creature on one half, a spell on the other, two costs printed. That is not decoration. Heaven's Gate cheats Light blockers into play for free, so the deck wants to be full of blockers; Twinpacts let those same slots also be spells when you draw them at the wrong time. It is a deck built to have no dead cards.

The other thing worth noticing: nothing costs four, five or six except the Gate itself. Two-cost search, then a jump straight to eight and nine. The deck does not intend to play fairly up the curve.

And four coming back

The 2025 products return alongside them: Mono-Water Liquid People 水単リキッド・ピープル, Fire/Light Red Zone 火光レッドゾーン, Mono-Light Rosia Miradante 光単ロージア・ミラダンテ and Fire/Nature Molt NEXT 火自然モルトNEXT.

Which tells you something These were sold once and are being brought back. Preconstructed decks in PLAY'S are not permanent stock — they appear, they go, and they return when the publisher decides to. If one of these is the deck you want, the window matters.

Why this is worth a page

New Division rotates. All Division does not — it holds everything, which means a card pool stretching back to 2019 and every keyword the game has ever printed. It is also, for exactly that reason, the harder format to enter.

A new player can look at New Division and see a few dozen relevant cards. Looking at All Division is looking at the whole game at once. The problem these products exist to solve

A preconstructed deck answers that by handing you a finished thing. For an English speaker it answers a second question too — what a functioning deck in this format even looks like, when no English source lists one.

They are All Division only The official notice is explicit: these cannot be used in New Division as they come. Cards inside them that happen to be ND-legal are still ND-legal on their own; the deck as printed is not.
