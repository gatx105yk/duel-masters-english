The collaboration brought people back. Then the format met them.

Three days in, the complaints have changed. Before launch the argument was whether the collaboration cards were strong enough. Now it is about the difficulty of the event itself, the randomness of what you get for paying, and what happens to someone who installs the game for an anime tie-in and then queues into the current metagame.

What follows is reported volume and pattern from public posts, paraphrased. No individual is named. Engagement figures are given where they indicate how widely a view is shared.

It is working as a hook

Returning players Posts along the lines of: heard about the Railgun collaboration, opened the game for the first time in nine months. This is the collaboration doing exactly what a collaboration is for.

Whatever else follows, that part is not in dispute. The event is pulling lapsed players back and bringing new ones in.

The complaint that got shared most

New players pulled in by the collaboration are picking up Savvark to play their favourite character, and getting their shields burned down by Raid. It was never a beginner-friendly format to begin with, and leaving Raid unnerfed on top of that feels deliberate. Paraphrased. The most-shared take on the launch, by some margin.

The structural point underneath it: a collaboration is an acquisition tool, and the thing it acquires people into is a two-year-old competitive format. Someone arriving for Misaka Mikoto does not arrive with a metagame deck, and the ranked ladder does not know or care that they came for the anime.

Whether that is anyone's fault is a separate question. The posts making this point are not calling for the collaboration to be cancelled; they are asking why a balance patch did not ship alongside it — which is the same request that surfaced the week before launch.

The Legend Battle is harder than expected

Difficulty Multiple posts describing the event as having been made worse rather than easier. One reports the first opponent's higher difficulty tier repeatedly deploying a cost-8 finisher, and running roughly twelve Shield Triggers without hitting one.

The Legend Battle is the route to the one collaboration card that does not require a purchase. If it is difficult, the free path narrows.

This is a player perception, not a measured change — no difficulty adjustment has been announced, and there is no public record of what the previous event's numbers were.

What paying actually gets you

Random drops The prize cards come out at random. One player reports buying four Collab Sets and receiving two Misaka and two Shokuhou — no Kuroko, which was the reason for buying. Another lists their pulls and concludes that everything except Ga Ryuzaku is worthless, adding that the lack of any real incentive to spend is itself disrespectful to the source material.

That last framing is worth noting because it inverts the usual complaint. The objection is not that the game is asking for too much money — it is that the rewards are not good enough to be worth paying for, and that a licensed collaboration deserved better than that.

What this adds up to

The pattern across all of it: the collaboration is succeeding at bringing people in and struggling at keeping them comfortable once they arrive. Those are different problems with different owners, and the posts increasingly distinguish between them — praise for the tie-in itself, frustration aimed at the state of the format it drops people into.
