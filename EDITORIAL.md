# Editorial rules

Working notes for Duel Masters English.

---

## Two kinds of article

Everything falls into one of two buckets, and they have different rules.

| | **Announcement** | **Analysis** |
|---|---|---|
| Content | What was posted, and when | What it means |
| Source | Official notice or official X post | Multiple, cross-checked |
| Interpretation | **None** | The whole point |
| Examples | New set, prize cards, event dates | Hall of Fame breakdown, metagame |

Most posts are announcements. Announcements are where the errors happen, because they look easy.

---

## Announcement articles: report, do not conclude

**State what was posted. Do not state what it means.**

| Write this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "Posted 18 Aug with the tag #ヒーローズセット" | "Part of the Heroes Set" |
| "Four cards are listed in the notice" | "The set contains four cards" |
| "The notice does not state pack contents" | quoting a wiki as if official |
| "3.2万 views, 104 reposts" | "well received" |

Every error this site has made came from the second column.

### The three that actually happened

**Assigned a card to the wrong set.** Four cards were posted under the same hashtag over two days.
Three were in the official notice; one was not. The hashtag was treated as membership. It is not —
it is a hashtag.

**Wrong kanji in a card name.** 課 instead of 誣 in 《「誣」の頂 ウェディング・イノセンス》. Different
character, different meaning, and a reader searching the Japanese name finds nothing. Cause: the
name was retyped instead of copied.

**Wiki data presented as official.** The pack composition table came from dmwiki but sat under a
heading citing the official notice, which does not state it.

### Copy, never retype

Japanese card names, set names, event names: **copy the characters**. Never type them from reading.
If the source cannot be copied from, say the name could not be verified rather than approximating it.

### Say what the source does not say

If the official notice omits something — price, pack count, a date — write that it is not stated, and
attribute the number to wherever it did come from. A gap is information. Filling it silently is the
error.

---

## Community reaction: volume, not voices

- **Report scale.** Post counts, view counts, how many separate accounts.
- **Do not quote individuals.** Paraphrase at most, name no one.
- **Do not assess sentiment as fact.** "Several posts raise X" is reportable. "Players are unhappy"
  is a conclusion.
- Only describe a pattern when independent accounts raise the same specific thing. One loud post is
  not a trend.

---

## Analysis articles: different bar

These may interpret, but the interpretation must be the author's own.

Facts belong to nobody. **Analysis belongs to whoever wrote it.** A tier placement can be translated;
"this deck was solved before it was banned" is someone's finding and cannot be the spine of a piece
here.

**Test:** if the source article vanished, would this still have a point? If no, do not publish.

---

## Do not claim to be first

An article said its decklists were translated "for the first time in English." **That was never
checked.** Someone maintains an English patch for this game and posts deck content; assuming nobody
had done it before was an assumption dressed as a fact.

A claim about what does not exist needs the same verification as a claim about what does. Usually it
is not worth the trouble — the article read fine with the sentence removed. **Say what this site
has, not what others lack.**

---

## Rule numbers are looked up, not remembered

A rulings article cited **101.4a**, which does not appear to exist, and gave the comprehensive rules
version as 1.49 when 1.50 had been published four months earlier. It also described trigger
resolution backwards — saying a newly triggered effect waits, when the rule says turn-player effects
are handled ahead of whatever the non-turn player still has queued.

That last one changes outcomes. **Rule numbers and version numbers get verified against the PDF.**

---

## When a source has a ceiling, say what it is

X search returns six to eleven posts and stops. Scrolling does not load more — tested repeatedly,
both orderings, with and without date ranges.

**So "many players think" is unsupported here.** What replaces it: the engagement figure on a single
post, or the count actually checked. A post with 331,000 views is evidence by itself. Four out of
eleven is a countable observation. Neither requires pretending to a sample that does not exist.

---

## Data published as images

Decklists, build costs and metagame graphs are published as **images** by their authors. Reading
them means opening the page and enlarging the graphic — slow, but the numbers are exact once read,
and a 40-card list with a build cost is the most concretely useful thing to carry.

Never republish the image. Read it, translate the contents, cite the source.

---

## Match the patch's names, not your own

**There is exactly one English naming for this game's cards: the community patch.** Readers using it
see those names on screen. Inventing a second set of English names fragments the vocabulary of a
community small enough that it cannot afford that.

So where the patch has a name, use it. A character was published here as *Ga Rumiiz* on 13 September
and corrected to **Ga Ryumizu** the next day, because that is how the patch's maintainer spells it.

### Names only. Effects are ours.

**The line is between a label and a piece of work.**

A card name is a short identifier whose whole purpose is that everyone uses the same one. Matching it
costs the patch nothing and saves the reader from having to guess that two articles are about the
same card.

**Card text is different.** Somebody sat down and translated it. Lifting that is taking their work,
and it also produces worse writing here — a literal rules translation reads like a rules translation.

So: **describe what a card does, in plain English, in your own words.** Say that a creature mills
four and returns a Darkness card to hand; do not paste a translated ability line. That is what the
articles already do, and it is why they can say things like *"a taunt that wins most fights it is
dragged into"* — which no translation of the card would give you.

The same applies to the Japanese original: **do not reproduce the printed text either.** Read it,
understand it, write the explanation yourself.

### The convention, as observed from the maintainer's public posts

- **Romanise, do not translate.** Ga Ryumizu, Minogami, Balga — not attempts at meaning.
- **English loanwords stay English.** Oriotis Judge, not an invented transliteration of it.
- **Japanese fandom terms are left alone** where no English word fits.

### Keep the Japanese alongside regardless

```
Centuria Dragon (ケントゥリア・ドラゴン)
```

The patch cannot be consulted directly — it is distributed outside anything reachable from here — so
some names will be guesses. **The Japanese name is the fallback that always works**: it is what the
card databases are indexed by, and it does not change. When a name here turns out to disagree with
the patch, fix the English and leave the Japanese as it was.

## Counted is not the same as measured

A community site polls players on new cards before release. Those polls produce a number, and a
number looks like evidence. **It is not evidence of the thing it appears to be evidence of.**

```
BEANS ranked survey     304 games that were played        a measurement
Pre-release card poll   hundreds of people guessing       a forecast, counted
```

Both are numbers. Only one is about what happened. A card polling 3.00/5 tells you what players
**expect**, which is worth reporting and worth labelling as such. **Write "a poll of expectations",
never "an actual number".** That phrasing went out on this site and had to be corrected within the
hour.

The same test applies to anything with a decimal point in it: **ask what was counted.** Games, or
opinions?

### Quoting other people's aggregates

A single figure with the source named is a fact you may use. **Their table is their work.**

```
fine    "a Japanese community site polls new cards; this one sits at 3.00/5"
not     reproducing the poll for all sixteen cards
```

Same line as the card names: **short facts travel, assembled work does not.**

## Before publishing

1. **Every number.** Dates, counts, costs, power, player caps, prices.
2. **Every proper noun, character by character.**
3. **Terms that look translatable but are not.**
   - **Zero civilization** ゼロ文明 is not "colourless"
   - **Mana value** マナ is a separate field from **cost** コスト
   - Hall of Fame has five categories, not two
   - New Division and All Division are separate formats
4. **Where each claim came from.** Card shop? They sell cards. Editorial site? Their judgement.
5. **Anything unverified.** Say so. "Not independently verified" is a complete sentence.

### Re-check the sources in the last minutes, not just the article

**21 October 2026.** The seventh-collaboration article went live at 18:06 saying three promised
notices "had not appeared by 18:00 on 20 October". All three were published at **17:00 that day**.
The last check made here was at 16:56. Five fact-check passes ran between 18:25 and 18:59 and none
of them looked at the news index again, because every one of them was checking the article against
the sources already gathered.

Two rules out of it:

- **Never assert a negative past the time you last looked.** "Nothing had appeared by 18:00" is a
  claim about 18:00. If the last check was 16:56, the only sentence available is "nothing had
  appeared as of 16:56" — and that one is worth writing, because it dates itself.
- **Re-fetch the news index immediately before pushing.** An article about something that opens in
  two days sits exactly in the window where the publisher documents it.

Checking the article against the sources you hold cannot catch a source that arrived after you
gathered them. That is a different pass and it has to be the last one.

### Pass zero is a script, and it is not one of the passes

`python3 check.py` does the mechanical half: table cells against `data-nd-daily.json`,
description slots, canonical trio, tag balance, entities, the middle dot, links, sitemap.
**It runs in a quarter of a second, so it runs before every publish and again after every
edit.** On its first run it found a duplicated `</article>` on four October pages, which
six fact-check passes across the 25 and 26 October work had not.

**And on that same run it missed one, which is the more useful half of the story.** A
separate hand-written grep for the site's middle-dot convention found `&middot;` standing in
for U+30FB inside `G・G・G` on a page published the day before. The script has a check for
exactly that and it passed the page clean, because it read the raw file and the bug was
spelled as an entity. It now tests the decoded text, and `--selftest` carries both
spellings as separate mutations.

The lesson is not about entities. **A check you have not seen fail is a guess**, and this one
was written, shipped inside the same commit as the rule telling people to trust it, and was
blind to the exact error that had prompted it. Hence the first rule below.

**It does not replace a pass and it does not count as one.** Its docstring lists the errors
from this site's history it would have caught and the ones it would not, and the second list
is the expensive one: every superlative, every figure on the wrong side of a boundary, every
fact about the world. A clean run means the human passes get spent on reading and on the
sources instead of on arithmetic.

Two rules for the script itself, both from the four tests above:

- **Every check earns its place by failing on a real mutation.** `--selftest` breaks the
  repository seventeen ways and requires each break to be caught. Two checks have already
  failed that standard. One fired on 24 of 52 pages and every firing was house style, so it
  was deleted and the deletion is recorded in the code where it used to be. The other —
  the middle dot — passed when the error was present, and was fixed. **Add the mutation in
  the same commit as the check**, or the check is untested.
- **A noise filter is not a fix.** `check-baseline.json` holds the unwrapped Japanese already
  accepted on the older pages, so a forgotten `jp` span on a new page is visible. It is not
  regenerated to make a run clean; an entry is added only after deciding the text is right.

### Fact-check twice

| Article | Pass 1 | Pass 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Hall of Fame | 5 | 2 |
| Magic crossover | 3 | 3 |
| Lionel Krone | 5 | — |
| Heroes Set | 1 (critical) | 2 |

The second pass is not optional. It has never come back clean.

### Vary what each pass reads, or they all find the same nothing

The seventh-collaboration article took seven passes. Each found what the ones before it could not,
because each read the article a different way:

| Pass | What it reads | Found |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | structure and arithmetic | 4 |
| 2 | prose against the sources | 3 |
| 3 | internal number consistency | 1 |
| 4 | every Japanese string against its source | 2 |
| 5 | terminology against the rest of the site | 2 |
| 6 | **the article end to end, as prose** | a self-contradiction five passes had missed |
| 7 | **the sources again, fetched fresh** | the article's central claim was false at publication |

Pass 6 catches what surgical editing creates: every sentence individually correct, the paragraph
not. Pass 7 is the one above. Neither is possible by re-reading the article against notes.

### The weekly meta article has a publish-day order

Worked out for week four and worth repeating. Do these in order:

1. **Read the last day's chart.** Both halves of the image. It should sum to 100.0 — and
   if it does not, reconstruct the game counts before assuming a misread, because rounding on a
   small sample can leave it short (22 October: 99.6 at 46 games, and correct).
2. **Add it and run the whole-series sum check**, not just the new day.
3. **Recompute every superlative in the piece against the finished week.** The last day can take an
   extreme off the week or add one — on 21 October it moved Cotton Zenith's floor from 3.6 to 2.0
   and turned the week's ending into a tie.
4. **Check the previous week's superlatives.** Any that this week passed get a dated forward note
   on that page and a section here. Never rewrite the older page: it was true of its own week.
5. **Re-fetch the news index before pushing.**
6. **Check all four description slots agree.**

**Do not publish a short week.** Week four could have run on six days two days earlier; the seventh
day changed two of its findings and its headline.

### Fact-check the supporting files too, not just the article

**Twice on 21 October the article came out clean and a file beside it did not.** The calendar
carried 栞葉るり spelled with 栾, and later the same day it said the changed card was behind
"the deck leading this site's daily readings for most of the week before it" — three days to
ジョーカーズ, two to 赤白レイド, one to バラギアラループ. The article printed the week's leaders in
its own table and claimed only the whole-format figure, so the passes run on the article could
not have found either.

The cause is the same both times: the calendar entry, the TODO note and the data record get
written early, from a partial view, and then the fact-checking all goes to the article.

**So when a session touches several files, list them before the last pass and check each one
against the finished article.** Anything the article states and a supporting file contradicts is
an error in one of them, and it is usually the file that was written first.

### These files count as touched, and nothing checks them

**24 October made the point the other way round.** The published article was clean through every
pass; the two errors were in `DATA.md` and `TODO.md`, written hours earlier in the same session.

- `DATA.md` gained a chart-reading check — "the colours run in descending order with `その他` last"
  — that was **written from inference**, which `CLAUDE.md` lists among the lines that do not move. It
  came from glancing at a few October charts. It cannot be verified against the series at all,
  because draw order is a property of the image and the data file records reading order; one day
  has no `その他`; and a missed slice leaves the rest descending, so it would not catch what it
  claimed to catch.
- `TODO.md` ended up with two sections on the same subject, because a new one was inserted above
  the old instead of replacing it. The survivor still said the story was unwritten and to wait for
  a reading that had arrived hours before.

**An article is wrong until someone corrects it. A rule is wrong until someone notices, and in the
meantime it is followed.** That is the longer-lived mistake of the two.

So the list of files to check before the last pass **includes the instruction files**, and a rule
added to one of them gets the same treatment as a sentence in an article:

1. **Say what it is built on.** A rule from three observations says so in the rule.
2. **Try to break it before writing it.** Run it against the whole series, not the case that
   suggested it. If it cannot be run against anything, that is itself the finding, and it belongs
   in the text.
3. **Ask what it would catch.** A check that stays true when the error happens is not a check.
4. **Replacing a section means deleting the old one.** Grep the file for the subject afterwards and
   count what comes back.

### A figure either side of a boundary belongs to one side of it

**26 October 2026.** A dated note added to the card-change page that morning said the Raid-less
build had a best of "4.3 on the nine days it appeared **before the change**". The 4.3 is from
22 October, which is *after* it — play resumed at 16:00 that day under the new card text, and
**the same page says so two paragraphs lower down**. Eight of the nine days are before the change
and their ceiling is 3.6. The 4.3 was also the weaker figure: two games on a 46-game day, against
3.6 being six games out of 166.

Built on one case, so here is the test run against the rest of the series rather than the case that
suggested it. **The week pages pass.** Week four spans the ranked-season boundary of 15/16 October and
says in its own note box that the 15th is the only day on the old ladder, naming both seasons. Week
three dates the old season's close against the day before it. The series' two boundaries — the season
change and the card change — are handled correctly everywhere they appear in an article.

**They failed only in a dated note bolted onto an older page.** That is the pattern, and it has a
cause: a note like that is written in minutes, from the two or three figures in front of you, outside
the publish-day order that forces a recount. The note stack on the card-change page took one such
figure three times and only the third phrasing was false, because the first two said "earlier" and
"before that" — both true — and the third said "before the change", which is a claim about a date.

So: **when a window has a boundary in it, every figure in the sentence gets asked which side it is on,
and the answer goes in the sentence or the figure comes out.** What this catches that nothing else did:
the arithmetic was right, the data file was right, the article passes were all run on the article. The
one thing that would have caught it is reading the note against the page it was added to.

A corollary worth keeping: "earlier", "previous" and "before that" are cheap and true. **"Before the
change" is a dated claim and costs a check.** Prefer the vague word unless the precise one has been
verified.

### The sitemap is a supporting file, and it was five pages short

Same day, found while adding the new article. The sitemap had 47 URLs against 52 published pages:
three October articles, the new one, and **`2026-08-18-lionel-krone.html`, which holds the best average
position on the site and had never been in it**. Nothing was checking it, because nothing reads a
sitemap after writing it.

**Add the entry in the same commit as the article**, and when touching the file, run the diff both
ways — published pages not in the sitemap, and sitemap URLs with no page. It is a set difference and
takes a second; reading the file does not catch an absence.

## When the publisher disagrees with itself

The rule for two secondary sources disagreeing — say so, name both, pick neither — applies with more
force when both sources are official. **A later notice is not automatically right. It is later.**

**21 October 2026**, two cases in one product:

- The X post of 19 October names 《守護水龍 ハイドランジア》 as one of 栞葉るり's two collaboration
  cards. The product notice of 20 October puts 《涼夏妖精カクラン》 in that slot and does not mention
  ハイドランジア anywhere, in either notice. For the other four livers, X and the notices agree exactly.
- The 19 October notice says the playmat and protect are **sold in the shop**. The 20 October notice
  calls them a **first-purchase bonus** on the deck builders.

Record which source said which and leave the earlier claim visible rather than overwriting it. The
reader who saw the X post needs to find it addressed, not deleted.

## Primary sources, in order of authority

| Source | Use for |
|---|---|
| `dm.takaratomy.co.jp/card/detail/` | Card text, cost, power, civilization, race, illustrator |
| `dm.takaratomy.co.jp/rule/regulation/` | Hall of Fame status. Overrides everything |
| `dmps.takaratomy.co.jp/news/` | PLAY'S announcements — the authoritative version |
| `@dmps_info` / `@t2duema` | Often ahead of the site. Treat as "posted", not "confirmed" |

**X posts are evidence that something was posted.** They are not evidence of what it belongs to.
When X and the official site disagree, or the site is silent, say so in the article.

---

## Card text and images

Never reproduce printed card text — describe the mechanics in plain English.
Never host card artwork. Embed official posts rather than copying them.

---

## Corrections

Fix in place, and say what changed. A silent edit is worse than the original error. Corrections go
in a labelled box at the foot of the article, dated.

---

## Order of work

1. Find the story
2. Gather from Japanese sources
3. Write it
4. **Fact-check against primary sources** — expect to find something
5. **Fact-check again** — expect to find something else
6. Fix, note anything still unverified
7. Publish
