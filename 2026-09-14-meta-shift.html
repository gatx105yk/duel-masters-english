The gap at the top has closed

On 5 September the most-played deck led the field by 7.7 points. On 13 September it led by 2.0. Nothing was banned and nothing was released — one deck simply climbed.

Zenith Heaven's Gate is the deck that moved. It has gone from third to second, and the top three now sit within five points of each other rather than spread across eight. All three decklists are here.

The Grand Prix does not explain it

The event ran on 13 September and was won by Magic Tools — which sits sixth on the ladder and moved by less than a point. Whatever effect a tournament result has on what people queue up with, it had not happened yet on the day itself.

Zenith Heaven's Gate had already been climbing before the final was played. If the win moves anything, it will show in the days after.

How much to read into this

One day, and a smaller one The 13 September survey covers 304 games against 420 a week earlier — about 28% fewer. A smaller sample moves more on its own, and Grand Prix day is exactly when you would expect fewer people on the ladder.

A 4.6-point move on 304 games is roughly fourteen matches. That is a real change and it is also not a large number of games. Treat the direction as informative and the exact figure as approximate.

The honest version: the deck at the top is less dominant than it was, and the deck that gained is the slowest of the three. Whether that holds needs another few days of data, which arrive daily.
