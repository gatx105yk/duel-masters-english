Chōtenkun takes BATTLE ARENA, and the pack news came out around it

BATTLE ARENA 2026 AUTUMN · 21 November, online broadcast
ちょうてんくん@ team漆黒
Announced 21:45 JST · the winning deck is not named in any post

The final was broadcast on 21 November from 17:00 JST and the winner was announced at 21:45. What the account has not published is the deck — not the winner’s, not anyone’s.

The announcement

【ついに決着！】
『BATTLE ARENA 2026 AUTUMN』 の
頂点に輝いたのは･･･！
ちょうてんくん@ team漆黒 選手！！

Reported as the account writes it. The player’s handle is ちょうてんくん with @ team漆黒 after it, a space after the at-sign included. The ellipsis is three half-width katakana middle dots, U+FF65 each, not an ellipsis character and not the full-width dot used elsewhere — which is the sort of thing that decides whether a search for the sentence finds it.

The post drew 42 reposts, 193 likes and 76,628 views, read at 11:35 JST on 24 November. The two card reveals in the same broadcast were read in the same pass and both are far above it: 292,490 and 1,047,504, the second being the most-viewed reveal of the run. The other four posts of that evening were not read — the special video, the release date, the collaboration MV and the closing post — so this page does not say the result was the least-viewed thing the account posted that night. It says it was far below the two things it has measured against.

The deck is not reported here, for two reasons

The first is that it is not published. Three posts carried 【環境デッキ紹介！】 — a segment on decks doing well in the tournament, ranked match and official events — but they name no deck in their text, and the content was on screen. Their times were not recorded in this pass, so this page does not place them inside the broadcast; they are not in the table below, which starts at the result. Nothing in the account’s posts says what the winner played.

The second is that this site does not reproduce decklists, so even where a list exists elsewhere it is not copied here. Naming an archetype from an official source is fine; this site has done that for a Grand Prix, where the publisher named the deck. Here it did not.

The prediction campaign

A campaign ran alongside, posted on the 19th and again on the 20th: predict the winner, and 100 correct predictors are drawn for an SR ticket. Entry was through the tournament site. This page has not read the campaign’s terms, so it does not say who was eligible — the publisher’s other campaigns this month have required residence in Japan, and that should be assumed to apply until the terms are read rather than because they were.
