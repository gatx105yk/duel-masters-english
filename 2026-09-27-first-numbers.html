The loop deck quadrupled in three days and fell on the fourth

This site wrote on 25 September that the first reading of the set 37 format did not exist yet, anywhere. It exists now, it is four days long, and the fourth day undid a third of the third. Here is what it says, with every date attached.

What the numbers are

A player posting as @sumamame logs the decks they face in ranked play, and the tally is published through the strategy blog BEANS, one report per day, split by division. It is the same survey, by the same method, that this site used for the old format, which is what makes the two comparable. This is an encounter rate: how often you sat across from a deck. It is not a win rate, and nothing here says any of these decks is good.

Baraghiara Loop went 3.7 to 9.7 to 15.0, and then to 10.4. It quadrupled in three days and gave back a third of that on the fourth.

15.0% was a peak, not a trend. When this page first went up on 27 September it had three points and described a climb; the report published the next morning turned the climb into a spike. That is the single most useful thing on this page, and it is a warning about the other numbers on it.

The source calls it a loop deck The label in the tally is バラギアラループ — Baraghiara Loop. Not the creature, not a colour combination. The deck is being recorded under what it does.
Nothing here is first place any more either Water Magic Tools led on 24, 25 and 26 September and then fell to 13.7% on the 27th, behind Fire/Light Raid at 15.8%. The format has now had two different most-played decks in four days.

And the share the survey does not label at all — its "other" bucket — has grown every single day: 8.5%, 12.7%, 15.1%, 16.4%. More of this format is off the edge of the chart each day than the day before. That is the opposite of a format settling down.

Which settles something this site got wrong

The day-one page carried three player posts saying the Baraghiara deck ran itself out of cards and that they could not work out what it was for. It also carried the other half of the conversation, where players called it the strongest thing in the set. Those posts were from 25 September, when the deck was at 9.7%.

On the day players were saying it does not work, it was already the fourth most common deck in the format, and it was half again as common the next day. Day one is not a measurement

The complaint and the climb are both real. People were describing their own games accurately and the format was moving underneath them. It has since moved back: the deck they could not make work is no longer climbing either. That is what a four-day-old format looks like, and it is the argument for not publishing a verdict during one.

The Grand Prix deck is not here

Water/Darkness Magic Tools won the Grand Prix on 13 September, eleven days before the set landed. It was never a common deck: the same survey had it at 6.2% and then 6.9% in the old format, sixth on the ladder on the day it won. In this sample it is not broken out as its own slice on 24 or 25 September, and is 3.0% on the 26th — and it is not broken out again on the 27th. On the days it is missing it is either absent or sitting inside the unlabelled "other" share; this data cannot tell the two apart, and it would be wrong to say the deck vanished.

The mono-Water build is the one at the top — 26.8%, then 20.5%, then 19.5%, first place on all three days. This is the same split the strategy site files under two different formats, with the Darkness version attached to the format that ended on 24 September. Yesterday this site could only say the two were probably different decks. Three days of encounter data say the newer one replaced the older one immediately.

And the Fire line was never missing

The day-one page said the set's new Fire cards went unmentioned in all twelve posts it read, and that this was a third of the set's coverage going undiscussed. Fire decks are 32.9%, 28.3% and 27.0% of the first three days — between a quarter and a third of the format.

The fourth day cannot be added to that line On 27 September the survey stops counting Fire in two buckets and starts counting it in five, splitting out variants it had not named before. Those five add to 32.4%, which looks like a jump — but the categories changed underneath the number, so it is not the same measurement.

This is worth stating plainly because it is the kind of thing that produces a fake trend: a survey that reclassifies mid-week will show movement that no player caused.
These are two different claims, and only one of them is settled Fire is plainly being played. None of the twelve posts named Raid, and Raid is the second most common deck on all three days.

But an encounter tally records which deck you faced, not which cards were in it. Whether those Fire decks are running set 37's new Fire cards is not something this data can show, and both of these archetypes existed before the set. The day-one page's actual claim — that the new Fire cards were not being talked about — is still open.
Raid barely noticed the new set The last reading of the old format tracked Raid at 19.8%, then 17.8%, then 17.9%. It is 16.5% on 26 September, in a format that rotated a set out and added ninety cards. The players who said nothing had changed were right about this deck.

What this does not tell you

  • These are encounter rates, not win rates. A deck can be everywhere and lose.
  • One collector, all rank bands mixed. Master and Legend games are pooled with everything else, so this does not describe the top of the ladder.
  • 82 games on 24 September is a very small sample, and the hours after a maintenance are not a normal day. Treat that column as a direction, not a figure.
  • No tier list exists. The Japanese strategy site still prints 第37弾調査中 — "set 37, under investigation" — in the tier field of every new-format deck page as of 27 September.
  • The source's own categories are not fixed. Decks that were one bucket on Friday were five on Sunday, so a figure is only comparable to the days that counted it the same way.
  • Four days is four days, and the deck this page is named after has already gone up and come back down inside them.

When the next reading is

This site will re-measure on 3 October 2026, at nine days old, and by then there will be enough points to say whether 27 September was Baraghiara Loop being answered or simply a quiet Sunday.

The day-one page asked whether "nothing has changed" was a mood or a fact. Four days in, the honest answer is that the question is too early. Raid has not moved all week and is now top of the ladder without gaining a point. The deck the set is named after quadrupled and then fell. And a sixth of the format is in a bucket nobody has named.

Corrected 27 September, shortly after publication Three things were tightened. This page said the Grand Prix deck "does not appear at all" on 24 and 25 September — the survey does not break it out on those days, which is not the same as it being absent, because it may sit inside the unlabelled "other" share.

It also treated "Fire decks are a quarter of the format" as settling whether set 37's new Fire cards were being ignored. Those are different claims: an encounter tally records which deck you faced, not which cards were in it.

And the collector's method is now described as what can be seen — logged ranked games — rather than "every ranked game".
