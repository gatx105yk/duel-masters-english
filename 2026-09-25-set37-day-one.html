Day one: nobody is talking about the cards I wrote about

The set 37 coverage on this site read the cards as three lines: Fire emptying its hand, Darkness and Water banking things, Jokers countering the spells. A day after release, one of those three is what people are playing.

Twelve posts, one day in This is not a measurement, and no ranked survey covering the new format exists yet. What it is: the first thing people said after actually playing, paraphrased and anonymised.

What people are playing

Named repeatedly Water Magic Tools and Baraghiara. One post opens by saying those two are what everyone is talking about, and then announces they are playing something else entirely.
Named once each Ga Ryumizu, Balga Jockey, dragon ramp. One player who opened forty packs and pulled four copies of the two-cost field says plainly that they are building around what they got, and that they pulled nothing Baraghiara-related at all.

The Fire line does not appear in any of the twelve. Not the hero, not the empty-hand payoffs, none of it. That was a third of what this site wrote about the set.

The deck that got stronger was already the best deck

Water Magic Tools is not new. It is the deck that won the Grand Prix eleven days before the set arrived, and the new Madougu cards feed directly into it.

A set that reinforces the reigning tournament deck does not change the format. It settles it. What "nothing has changed" might actually mean
Two posts, flatly That the format has not changed, and that it is dull. No elaboration in either.

Hold that lightly. Day-one disappointment is a genre, and a set that rotates a year-old release out at the same time is going to move numbers whatever the new cards do. The ranked data will say.

And the card the set is named after is not working

Three separate posts That the Baraghiara deck runs itself out of cards; that a player could not work out what its strength was supposed to be; and that its effect triggering on summon rather than elsewhere was not the upgrade an existing card wanted.

This site described those Twinpacts as the raw material for the set's alternate win condition — cast five spell halves in a turn and a loss becomes a win. That reading was about what the cards say. It did not consider whether a deck built to do it survives doing it.

Which is the difference this site keeps writing about Card text tells you what is possible. It does not tell you what a deck does to itself on the way there. A win condition that needs five casts in a turn needs a deck that can find five cards in a turn, and the reports are that it cannot.

What I got wrong, and what I will know in a week

The three-line reading of the set was built from card text, and as a description of what the cards do it still holds. As a prediction of what people would play, one line out of three landed.

This page said, before release, that reactions to an unreleased set are reactions to card text and reactions to a released one are reactions to losing. That turned out to be the right distinction and I am on the wrong side of it — everything written here so far was the first kind.

The last reading of the old format is here, taken the day before release. The first reading of the new one arrives in a few days, and that is the one that settles whether "nothing changed" is a mood or a fact.
