PLAY'S · News · 13 Sep 2026

The rival won

Channabe won, reportedly with Water/Dark Magic Tools, beating Dapico in the final. The official column had named exactly those two decks two days earlier — but it expected the other one to take it.

And during the broadcast, Takara Tomy put up a teaser for the next set. Its name points at a paper release from 2018.

The column called the matchup and missed the result

Kono Masanari's pre-event piece named Dapico as the deck with the fewest weaknesses and expected it to convert best at the top tables. He named Water/Dark Magic Tools as its main rival. Both reached the final. The rival won.

Which is a better outcome for the point this site keeps making than a clean prediction would have been. On the ranked ladder in early September, Raid was the most-played deck at 19.8% and Dapico second at 12.1%; Magic Tools sat sixth, at 6.2%. Those figures are here.

The most-played deck did not win. The deck the analyst expected to win did not win. The deck that won was sixth in play rate. What gets brought, what gets predicted, and what gets there
On the winning deck specifically That Channabe piloted Water/Dark Magic Tools is reported by a Japanese TCG news blog covering the stream, and is not yet confirmed by an official announcement. That the final was Dapico against Magic Tools, and that Channabe won it, is corroborated across independent posts. If the official result says otherwise, this page will be corrected again.

The reaction

Paraphrased from public posts in the half hour after the final. No individual is named beyond the winner, who is the subject of the news.

The final itself Repeated descriptions of a comeback game — people reporting they shouted at the screen, one saying they burst into tears without quite understanding why. Several posts call it the best match they have watched.
Players who went out early A striking amount of good humour from people who dropped at 1-3 or 3 losses. One, who had played Dapico himself, wrote that watching the final showed him what the deck could really do, and signed off with next time I win.
Lapsed players More than one post along the lines of: this makes me want to play again. That is the same effect the collaboration had three weeks ago, from a completely different direction.
The room emptying One observer noted the chairs visibly thinning out over the course of the day — a 3,500-player single-elimination bracket does that — while the noise from those who stayed came through on the broadcast.

Set 37

Teaser only — no date, no cards 『虹帝∞命動 -FINAL ORAORA RINGFINITY!!-』

Tagline: let the circling rage break the world 廻る怒りよ、世界を壊せ. A second post carries a line of dialogue — gather beneath my rage. The teaser has 300,000 views. There is no page for it on the official site yet.

The name is doing the work here. "OraOra Ringfinity" is lifted from a paper set released in 2018 — DMRP-08, the closing release of the Twinpact Series, built around the boss character Baraghiara.

The teaser shows the cards

It runs to just over two minutes, and around the eighty-second mark it puts up five Twinpacts of the same creature, side by side. They are not five different creatures — all five are Nature creatures with identical stats, cost 9 and power 17000. What changes is the spell printed on the other half.

Four follow one naming pattern and the fifth does not. The odd one out is named after the phrase the Buddha is said to have spoken at birth — above heaven and below heaven, I alone am honoured — with one word swapped so that the cycle of rebirth is the thing standing alone.

These are reprints

The paper originals are from December 2018 All five appeared in DMRP-08, the fourth Twinpact Series expansion, released 21 December 2018 and long out of print. Same names, same five-way split.

Their shared keyword is written 輪廻∞ and read Rinfinity — it lets you cast the spell half while the creature stays in play, paying the cost, with an effect that scales off how many copies you control. That is where the set's English name comes from. "RINGFINITY" is not a coinage for the teaser; it is a keyword ability from 2018.

Which tells you something about what set 37 is. PLAY'S rebuilds the paper game's back catalogue a few years behind, rebalancing as it goes. This is the 2018 finale arriving in the digital game, eight years later — and the word FINAL in the title is doing the same job it did then.

What the 2018 cards actually do

The creature half is the same on all five. Described rather than quoted:

Shared creature side Nature, cost 9, power 17000, Master Hazard / Grand Sect, T-Breaker.

When summoned, it untaps your entire mana zone — so casting it does not end your turn. And Rinfinity lets you cast the spell half while the creature stays on the board, paying the cost normally; doing so ramps you one card for every copy of the creature you control.

That second ability is the engine. Each copy makes the next spell cheaper to afford, which is what the wheel in the name refers to.

The expensive one is the weakest. Japanese analysis from 2021 makes the point that a cost-8 spell fights the Rinfinity engine rather than feeding it, and that giving an entire board World Breaker is overkill when three shields are usually enough. It is also the only single-civilization card of the five, which is why it is the one that still costs money.

The whole set is named after a wheel 輪廻 is samsara, the cycle of death and rebirth. The tagline is circling rage. The set is Ringfinity — ring plus infinity — and carries an ∞ in its Japanese title. None of that survives translation, which is why the English name looks like noise and the Japanese one does not.

The teaser also shows two new cards resolving as Shield Triggers, one Nature and one Darkness, neither named on screen. It closes with coming soon and no date.

Timing, if the usual cadence holds: PLAY'S releases a numbered set roughly every two months, and the current one landed on 23 July. Release calendar.

The reaction: will it even work here?

Paraphrased and anonymised from public posts within two hours of the teaser. Engagement figures given where a view was widely shared.

Delight, first A lot of straightforward pleasure — people saying they love this card and have wanted it in the digital game. Several noticed that the teaser appears to show all five arriving together, the same way the paper set distributed them.
The board limit · the substantive worry PLAY'S caps how many creatures can be in the battle zone. Paper does not. Rinfinity scales off how many copies of the creature you control, so the deck wants to stack them — and that is exactly what the digital game's limit prevents. One post, widely shared, says they are excited but nervous, and asks whether the developers can really be planning to bring it across with no adjustment at all.
A blunter version of the same thing Another, similarly shared: this card's abilities do not seem suited to PLAY'S — is it coming as printed?

This is a real question, not a complaint. PLAY'S rebalances rather than ports, and has rewritten card text before. A mechanic built on having many copies of a nine-cost creature in play at once is a genuine test of that.

Someone noticed the timing One post puts it as: this month PLAY'S gets five Baraghiara and paper gets a sixth. That is accurate — the paper game reveals a new Baraghiara Twinpact of its own for the set arriving 19 September, six days after this teaser. Its spell half is all five civilizations at once.
And immediately, speculation about what else is in it Other names from the same era started circulating within the hour — and a running joke that a set with five of the same character must mean a quintuplets anime collaboration is next.

Also announced

Ultimate All Division Deck 2026 — a preconstructed deck for the non-rotating format, built on Fire/Light Apollonus. Its card list was shown during the broadcast. All Division gets far less attention than New Division, so a supported precon for it is worth noting.

And a giveaway: ten free packs through the official web shop, framed as thanks for the current set selling well.
