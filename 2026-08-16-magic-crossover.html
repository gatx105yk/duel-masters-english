Black Lotus is a Duel Masters card

So are Emrakul, Nicol Bolas, Jace, Serra Angel, Library of Alexandria and Brainstorm. Fourteen Magic cards have been printed as Duel Masters cards, in frames designed to imitate Magic's old border, and almost no one outside Japan knows they exist.

You cannot buy them in English. They have never had an English printing, because Duel Masters has not had one since 2006.

The list

The crossover cards first appeared in DMEX-08「謎のブラックボックスパック」 (Mystery Black Box Pack, April 2019), a deliberately absurd novelty set, and were reprinted as a group in DMEX-18「20周年超感謝メモリアルパック 裏の章 パラレル・マスターズ」 (Parallel Masters, the 20th anniversary reverse chapter) in frames imitating Magic's old border. Emrakul specifically has since been reprinted twice more, in DM23-EX3 and DM26-EX2, so it is not a one-off curiosity — it stays in circulation.

Parallel Masters shipped on 19 February 2022, six cards a pack, at 330 yen. Takara Tomy's own product page frames the crossover section as a counterfactual: the manga began twenty years ago with characters duelling in Magic, so what if Duel Masters had just carried on being Magic? The page states the design goal outright — the cards faithfully recreate the original Magic cards.

A Japanese card shop's write-up of Parallel Masters called fourteen an unprecedented number — earlier special sets had included one or two collaboration cards at most. The shop put them in its Duel Masters display case specifically so Magic players who do not play Duel Masters could find them.

How the rules got translated

This is the interesting part, and the reason these are worth more than novelty. Duel Masters and Magic share ancestry but not mechanics, so every card had to be rebuilt. Emrakul is the clearest example.

Annihilator is the elegant one. In Magic it strips permanents. Duel Masters has no single equivalent zone, so it takes six cards from anywhere the opponent has face-up cards — creatures, shields, mana. In a game where mana is cards you have permanently spent and shields are your life total, that is arguably worse than the original.

The Zero civilization detail is worth pausing on. Duel Masters has five civilizations plus Zero, which is its own thing rather than an absence — Magic's colourless is the closest analogue but not an equivalent. Emrakul being Zero rather than, say, Darkness is a deliberate placement.

Eldrazi is a real race in Duel Masters and Emrakul is the only creature that has it. It is paired with Zenith, an existing high-end Duel Masters race, so the card sits inside the game's own taxonomy rather than beside it.

The card itself, art included, is on the official database: Emrakul, the Aeons Torn (DMEX-08 91). Worth opening if you want to see what a Magic Eldrazi looks like rendered by a Duel Masters illustrator.

The girl standing next to Emrakul

A post about the Duel Masters Emrakul passed 680,000 views on X in June. The reaction was not about the crossover — that was treated as old news. It was about the picture. Roughly: I get that Emrakul went to Duel Masters, there's precedent. Why is she a girl?

The answer came in the replies, and it is worth knowing because the same confusion will happen to any Magic player who encounters Duel Masters art without context.

Emrakul was not redesigned. The girl is a different character. Emrakul is assigned to Zenith （ゼニス）, the Zero-civilization creature group. The girl in the image is Crista （クリスタ） — a human character from the manga who is the story's primary Zenith user. She is drawn alongside the creature because that is her creature group, the same way the anime's other protagonists appear next to theirs.

This pairing is a standard convention in Duel Masters promotional art, not a one-off. The official 25th anniversary tour handed out postcards built entirely on it — Win with Jashin-tei, Crista with Chris = Tabula = Rasa, Kurojo Kyoushirou with Baromea Lady. Character on one side, their signature creature on the other.

Crista's own creature, Chris = Tabula = Rasa 《クリス=タブラ=ラーサ》, is a Zero-civilization Zenith at cost 18 and power 28482, from DM23-EX3 — which is also one of the sets Emrakul was reprinted into. They sit in the same corner of the game.

Emrakul itself is unchanged. The DMEX-08 card is a cosmic-horror Eldrazi illustrated by Ittoku. The Parallel Masters reprint puts it in an imitation of Magic's old card frame, and Takara Tomy's own product copy says the crossover cards faithfully recreate the original Magic cards.

Worth adding, since the original poster raised it: Emrakul is female in Magic too. Not implied — Wizards' own flavour text uses "she" throughout. Gazing upon her is to gaze upon the abyss. Nothing about that changed in translation either.

Separately, there is an actual anthropomorphisation line

Not this card, but it exists and it is large: 「ドラゴン娘になりたくないっ！」 — "I Don't Want to Become a Dragon Girl!" — reimagines the game's dragons as schoolgirls and has its own sets, most recently DM26-EX3 on 8 August 2026. If you have seen a Duel Masters creature genuinely redrawn as an anime character, that is probably where it came from.

Why this exists at all

Wizards of the Coast designed Duel Masters. It launched in Japan in 2002 as a stripped-down Magic — no lands, no life total, five face-down shields — and it worked. The English version was discontinued in 2006; the Japanese game is in its twenty-fourth year and remains one of Japan's three largest card games.

So a Magic crossover in Duel Masters is not licensing between strangers. It is one Wizards game visiting another, printed by Takara Tomy, in a language most Magic players cannot read, for a market they cannot buy from.

Emrakul also appeared as a 3D model in the Duel Masters anime — episode 40 of Duel Masters King, broadcast 7 February 2021.
