Set 37 is running two opposite ideas at once

At least thirty-two cards have been shown so far, and the ones whose text has been published fall into three lines, two of which share a shape and one of which does the opposite. One empties your hand and spends your own shields. The other stacks cards up under a two-cost field until it can cast a ninety-nine cost spell for free.

Effects are described below in plain English; the official posts are embedded so you can see the cards themselves.

Line one: Fire, and running out on purpose

Every Fire card shown pays you for having less — fewer cards in hand, fewer shields in front of you.

Seven mana becomes two if you are willing to take the hit — and the shield you spent is what switches on the bonus. The cost and the payoff are the same action. The condition reads any player, so an opponent breaking your shields does the job as well.

《スケボー・チュリス》 · Common
Type CreatureRace Beat Jockey Cost 3Power 2000
On playChoose one: discard a card and draw two, or destroy an opposing creature of power 2000 or less.

The small one that makes the rest work. Discarding to draw is how a Fire deck reaches an empty hand deliberately — and an empty hand is the condition everything else is waiting on.

A fourth Fire card, revealed separately through Corocoro, takes the same idea further: free to cast if it is the only card you are holding, then a board wipe. Its own page is here.

Line two: Darkness, and stacking up to ninety-nine

The cards shown on Grand Prix day do the opposite. Nothing is spent — it is banked.

Type SpellRace Dolsuzak Cost 99
EffectSummon up to four Dolsuzak from your hand or graveyard without paying for them. EffectTake an extra turn after this one — unless this already is one.
The ninety-nine is not decoration The field unlocks a Water spell costing 99 or less. This spell costs exactly 99. The number exists so that these two cards find each other, and no honest deck ever pays it.

So the sequence reads: two mana to place the field, spells tucked underneath as you cast them, and on the turn the fourth one lands, four free creatures and another turn. That is the shape of the Darkness line.

Two more from the same day

Melavolgal and Shiten no Sabaki 《紫天ノ裁キ》 were shown the same day. Both belong to the Darkness line; the first is a Dolsuzak, described in its post as destroying without regard for sides. I have not read either card's text and am not describing it.

Line three: Water, and a new keyword

Two Water cards arrived on 15 September, and with them an ability the game has not had before.

Zenoshark / Crystal Memory

《水晶の記録 ゼノシャーク／クリスタル・メモリー》 · Very Rare
Type TwinpactRace Mutopia Creature 3 / 1000Spell 4
Smash BurstWhen the creature attacks, cast this card's own spell half for free. Spell halfShield Trigger. Name a number, then Super Search your deck for a card of that cost and take it to hand.
"Super Search" is not the same as searching PLAY'S does not let you look through your whole deck. A search shows you up to three distinct cards matching the condition, chosen at random from those that qualify; a Super Search shows five. You pick from what you are shown.

In practice it is often a clean tutor — few decks run six or more different cards at the same cost, so naming a number usually surfaces what you wanted. But it is a cap, not a guarantee, and the paper version of this spell has no such limit.
Smash Burst changes what a Twinpact is Until now a Twinpact made you pick a half — creature or spell, not both. This keyword lets one card be both: a three-cost body that fires a four-cost tutor every time it attacks, and that still works as a Shield Trigger sitting in defence.

Clanvia

《不夜城艦 クランヴィア》 · Super Rare

Which is the Darkness field's idea again, on a creature. Spells accumulate underneath; attacking spends them. Two different civilizations have now been given the same shape.

Both directions happen. The Darkness SR above got cheaper and gained a keyword; this one lost precision. There is no rule that the digital version is kinder.

The Madougu spells that make the Darkness engine run

Three cheap Water spells were shown on the official video channel on Grand Prix day. They are the fuel for the two-cost field above — every one you cast goes underneath it.

CardCostWhat it does
Daju Gonpadu 堕呪ゴンパドゥ2Look at the top three, take one, rest to the bottom.
Daju Gyapudu 堕呪ギャプドゥ2Draw one. Super Shield Trigger: if it fires that way, no opposing creature can attack or block.
Daju Airvo 堕呪エアヴォ4Shield Trigger. Bounce anything costing seven or less.
Super Shield Trigger is a different keyword An ordinary Shield Trigger fires from any broken shield. A Super Shield Trigger has a further condition attached and does something larger when it is met — here, shutting down the opponent's whole board for a turn. Most of the time you cast the card normally and get the small effect.

The point of the cheap two-cost ones is speed. The field wants four spells underneath it before it does anything, so the deck runs ten or more cards that cost two and dig. Getting the field down by turn three is the whole opening.

And one of them shows the usual weakening not happening A recurring pattern when cards cross over is that they become opponent-only — a bounce spell that could hit anything in paper can only hit their side here.

The four-cost bounce above was not restricted that way. It can return your own cards, which matters because the field is a card you sometimes want back. It did lose something else, though: it now targets elements rather than cards, so the spells stacked under a returned field come back with it rather than going to the graveyard.

And Light gets a blocker that fetches blockers

Welkius

《闘門の精霊ウェルキウス》 · Super Rare
Type CreatureRace Angel Command Cost 9Power 7500
BodyBlocker, Double Breaker. TriggerOn arrival, and again whenever one of your creatures wins a battle: draw a card, then you may put a Blocker from your hand into play.

Nine mana, and no deck will pay it. A nine-cost Light Blocker is exactly what Heaven's Gate cheats into play for free — and this one, on arrival, puts another Blocker out behind it.

Which changes what that deck is doing The Nature/Light preconstructed deck currently plays the Gate as a ceiling: two big blockers, then win from behind them. This makes the Gate a chain instead. Cheat this in, it fetches a third; block with it and win the battle, it fetches a fourth.

The seven-card battle zone cap starts to matter at that point — a full board turns the next one into a discard.

The set is retelling a comic from 2018

Messer Schmitt

《メッサー・シュミット》 · Rare
Civ NatureRace Spriggan / Grand Sect Cost 8Power 8000+
BodyMach Fighter, Double Breaker, and +4000 power while fighting. UnusualYou may summon it out of your mana zone, not only from hand. On winningDraw a card. If it is the first battle it has won this turn, you may drag another of the opponent's creatures into a fight with it.
Three cards in this set are the same scene In the 2018 comic that ran alongside the paper release, Messer Schmitt is a legendary hunter who has been brainwashed, and he fights alongside the Darkness factory boss — the Twinpact further up this page.

He is beaten by the Jokers hero, and with his dying words hands over the power that turns that hero into his upgraded form — the creature shown in this set's teaser trailer.

Georg, Messer Schmitt and the upgraded Jokers hero are all in set 37. The set is not a pile of reprints from a period; it is a specific story, cast as cards.

Which is a thing English-speaking players have no access to. The comic has never been published in English, so the connective tissue between these cards is invisible unless somebody points at it.

What this set actually is

The Duel Masters Wiki's entry for it gives the cleanest one-line description anyone has managed: a set built mainly out of the preconstructed decks that were sold alongside a paper set from June 2018.

That explains a lot of what is above. The reanimating Darkness tribe, the Madougu spell engine, the contest-winning creature — these were not scattered cards, they were products, sold as decks, eight years ago. The digital game is bringing them across as a group.

Which is also why the differences matter so much here Precon cards are built to work with each other out of the box. Change one of them on the way across and the group behaves differently — and this set has changes in both directions, as the two notes above show.

What the split means

Two of the three lines win by stacking cards underneath something and spending them later. The third wins by having nothing left at all. Thirty-two cards in, this is the clearest thing about the set

The Darkness field banks spells under itself; the Water ship banks spells under a creature. Those are the same idea given to two civilizations. Fire is the one running the other way.

And none of them is what the set is named after. The headline cards are Nature — five reprints of a 2018 paper creature, built on stacking dragons under a Phoenix Field and casting off them. Those are covered here.

Four civilizations, three or four distinct plans depending on how you count. Worth holding loosely: thirty-two is still a fraction of a set, though the shape is firming up.
